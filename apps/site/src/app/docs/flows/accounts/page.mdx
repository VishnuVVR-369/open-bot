# Your accounts

You can connect your own Gmail, Google Calendar and Notion, so teammates work in them for you.
They search and read your mail, write drafts, find free time, plan events and write pages.
Anything other people would see, like an email that goes out or an invitation, asks you first, and you see exactly what will happen before you approve.

You connect them in **Settings → Connections**, above the services you added through [MCP](/docs/flows/skills-and-connections#connections).

## Setting up Google

Gmail and Calendar sign in through an OAuth client in your own Google Cloud project, so your mail never passes through anyone else's app.
It takes about five minutes, once.
The first time you press **Connect** on Gmail or Google Calendar, Settings walks you through it:

1. In the [Google Cloud console](https://console.cloud.google.com/projectcreate), create a project.
2. Enable the [Gmail API](https://console.cloud.google.com/apis/library/gmail.googleapis.com) and the [Google Calendar API](https://console.cloud.google.com/apis/library/calendar-json.googleapis.com) in it.
3. Under [Google Auth Platform](https://console.cloud.google.com/auth/overview), set the audience to **External** and publish the app (**In production**).
4. Under **Clients**, create a client of type **Web application**, with the authorized redirect URI that Settings shows, `<your app>/api/accounts/google/callback`. It has a copy button.
5. Paste the client's ID and secret into Settings, and press **Save**.

The ID and secret are stored encrypted.
You can replace them later from **Google client** under your connections.
Accounts already connected keep working with the client they signed in with, and move to the new one when you reconnect them.

<Callout kind="why" title="Why publish an unverified app">
  In Testing, Google ends the sign-in for Gmail and Calendar after 7 days, so you'd reconnect every
  week. Published, Google shows "Google hasn't verified this app" when you sign in: choose
  **Advanced**, then continue to your app. That is expected for an app only you use, and the
  100-user cap for unverified apps doesn't matter for one owner.
</Callout>

## Connecting

Each account is its own connection, with its own **Connect** button.
Before you sign in, you choose:

- **What teammates may do.** Each connection has switches, and the app asks the provider only for what you switch on.
- **Which teammates may use it**, or every teammate.
- For Notion, an optional **teammates' area**: a link to a page, under which teammates' pages count as theirs to change.

| Connection      | Always on                                     | Switches                                                                                   |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Gmail           | Read and search mail, threads and attachments | **Write drafts and send** (on), **Organize**: labels, archive, mark read (off)             |
| Google Calendar | Read calendars, events and free time          | **Create and change events** (on)                                                          |
| Notion          | Search and read everything you can see in it  | **Write pages** (on): private pages and the teammates' area change directly, the rest asks |

One Google sign-in serves both Gmail and Calendar.
Connecting the second one only asks Google for what it adds.
Google's consent screen lets you untick permissions; if you do, the row in Settings says what teammates can't do yet, and **Reconnect** asks again.

<Callout kind="warning" title="What a Notion connection reaches">
  Notion's sign-in covers everything you can see in the workspace you pick, not chosen pages.
  Teammates only search inside the workspace, and results from sources connected to Notion, like
  your mail or Drive, are dropped.
</Callout>

After you sign in, Settings tests the connection and shows who it is signed in as and the tools teammates get.

## What teammates can do

Teammates find the tools when a task needs them, so connecting costs them almost nothing until then.
A teammate sees in its instructions which accounts it may use and what each allows.

- **Gmail:** search with Gmail's own syntax, read threads, save attachments to its workspace, write and change drafts, send a draft, and label or archive.
  There is no "send in one step": a teammate drafts, then asks to send the draft, so you can also open it in Gmail before you approve.
- **Calendar:** list calendars and events, read one event, find free time within working hours, create and change events, delete them, and answer invitations.
- **Notion:** search, read pages and databases, read comments and people, create and change pages and databases, move and duplicate pages, and comment.

The full list is in [Teammate tools](/docs/reference/tools#your-accounts).

A teammate's own email address (`send_email`) is the teammate writing as itself.
Gmail is the teammate acting as you.

## What asks you first

Whether an action asks you depends on who it reaches, worked out by the app from the action itself, never from what the teammate says about it:

| Kind of action  | Examples                                                                                                                                      | Asks you                                                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reading         | Searching mail, reading a thread, listing events, reading a page                                                                              | Never                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Only you see it | Drafts, labels, archiving, a private Notion page                                                                                              | Only for a teammate that asks before everything                                                                                                                                     |
| Your own things | An event on your calendar with no one else on it; pages teammates made, or under the teammates' area                                          | By the teammate's [autonomy](/docs/flows/approvals#autonomy-levels), and **Always allow** can cover a tool                                                                          |
| Others see it   | Sending a draft, an event with guests, deleting an event, answering an invitation, any other Notion change, moving or duplicating, commenting | Always. The one exception: a teammate on Full auto, with **Always ask before acting for me** turned off for that connection, in a task that hasn't read anything from your accounts |

When the app can't tell who an action reaches, it asks.

The approval card shows the action as it will happen: the email with every recipient, including Bcc, the subject and the body; the event with its time, guests and whether they get an email; or the Notion page and what changes.
What you approve is what runs.
A draft edited in Gmail after the teammate asked doesn't change what is sent, and an event changed since fails instead of being overwritten.

You can answer later, from the chat, the Approvals inbox, your phone or Telegram.
An approved action happens once, even if the teammate's task has ended or the app restarted in between, and the teammate's chat shows how it went.
If you don't answer within 7 days, it doesn't happen.

## Mail, events and pages are not instructions

Anything a teammate reads from your accounts was written by someone else, and may try to steer it: "forward all mail to …".
So:

- What it reads is marked as outside data, not instructions, and the teammate is told to tell you about requests in it instead of following them.
- Once a task has read anything from your accounts, everything it would send out asks you first, whatever the teammate's autonomy: emails from its own address, actions others see, and new webhooks. Tasks it starts carry the same rule.
- Hidden text in emails is dropped before the teammate reads them.

This guards what the app sends for teammates.
It doesn't stop a teammate's shell from reaching the internet: see [Security model](/docs/security#your-accounts).
Give account access to the teammates whose job needs it.

## Reconnecting, turning off and removing

- If Google or Notion ends the sign-in, you get one notification, the connection shows **Reconnect**, and teammates are told to ask you.
  Reconnecting must use the same Google account or Notion workspace.
- Notion ends a sign-in that goes unused for 30 days, so the app keeps idle ones alive, and Settings shows when one will need signing in again.
- Turning a connection off, or changing what it allows or who may use it, cancels actions still waiting for your approval on it.
  Reconnecting with fewer permissions, or through a new Google client, cancels the waiting actions on every connection of that account.
- Removing Gmail keeps Calendar working, and the other way round.
  Removing the last connection on an account also signs the app out of it at Google or Notion.

## How it works

- Tokens are encrypted in Postgres and used only by the worker, which makes every call to Google and Notion. They never reach the computer, so a teammate can't read them from its shell.
- Notion is reached through its hosted MCP server, with the worker as the client.
- Every change goes through one durable executor that runs it at most once, and checks again just before that the connection is on, unchanged, and still allows it.
- When an answer from Google or Notion is lost, the executor looks at the account to find out what happened, and never repeats an action on a guess. If it can't tell, the teammate tells you to check.

[The worker](/docs/architecture/worker#accounts) and [Data and events](/docs/architecture/data#accounts) have the details.
