# The Android app

Your teammates on your phone: a home briefing, chats with live replies and attachments, search, the computer's screens, pages, the task board, automations, memory, the vault and budgets, and approvals you can decide from the notification shade.
It is an Expo app (React Native) that talks to your open-bot deployment through the same RPC contract the web app serves (`packages/api`).
It is installed over USB; there is no Play Store listing.
Building, configuring and testing it is covered in `apps/mobile/README.md`.

## Home and teammates

Home leads with what needs you, then your teammates, then a briefing of work finished while you were away.
Each teammate's avatar shows what they're doing: a soft pulse while they work, an amber ring and mark while they wait for you, and a dashed ring while they're up next.
Teammates who need you sort first; tap one to open your latest chat with them, or hold to open their profile.
A teammate's profile has Message, New chat and Screen, the instructions they suggest for themselves, their open tasks, their daily check-in and automations, how much they do on their own, their Always allow rules, their memory and lessons, what they spent this month, and every conversation they're in.
Use Computer to watch or take over a teammate's screen.

## Chats

Chats shows unread dots, previews and who is working; filter by Unread, Direct, Groups or Scheduled when there are any.
Hold a chat to mark it read or archive it.
Use New chat for a direct conversation or a group, and @-mention teammates to choose who answers.
A teammate names a new chat after its topic once that's clear, and the chat list then says whose reply it is; a name you give is never replaced.
The chat menu lets you rename or archive a conversation; archiving keeps its history.
Tap a chat's title to open the teammate's profile.
When a teammate is idle, the chat header, their profile and New chat show when they last worked, such as "Active 5m ago".

In a chat, a teammate's work folds into one line, such as "Worked for 21s · 5 steps", with the steps a tap away.
While they work, one line says what they're doing right now, and long command output folds after 14 lines.
Code scrolls sideways with a fade at the edge, and every code block has a copy button.
Unsent text is kept per chat on the phone, so leaving for an approval or another app doesn't lose it.

Attach a photo, take one with the camera, or pick a file from the composer.
Failed uploads can be retried or removed before sending.
Images, Markdown, text and CSV/TSV files open in the app; tables scroll in both directions.
PDFs and other formats open in a compatible app on the phone.
The microphone adds dictation to the current draft; leaving that composer cancels recognition.
The latest reply shows copy, read aloud and ratings under it; hold any reply, or use its More button, for the same actions plus its time and usage.
Feedback offers an option to remember a lesson or try again.
The reply's menu also has What they remembered: the saved memories that reply was given, each opening to read or edit, or saying it changed or was forgotten since.
You can steer a working teammate with another message, stop it, or retry a failed or stopped turn.

## Search and history

Use the header's search and notification buttons to find earlier work.
Search finds teammates, chats, messages and pages; links to a teammate's reply load older history before scrolling to it.
If loading history fails, use Retry to continue.
Loaded history stays in place when newer replies arrive.
A chat becomes read across devices after fresh history has loaded while it is visible; failed refreshes keep it unread.

## Approvals and notifications

Approvals supports Approve, Deny and Always allow, with saved rules one tap away at the top.
Requests that spend money, send email or delete things carry a short warning, in the chat and on the request itself.
Past decisions are grouped by day.
Notification approvals can be decided without opening the app; purchases use Review and require the phone's authentication check before approval.
Reply on a message notification sends directly to its teammate, including in a group chat.
A reply that isn't sent stays in the notification in full, says it wasn't sent, and opens its chat; opening the chat doesn't clear it, so you dismiss it once you've sent it again.

When a teammate's work takes longer than 20 seconds, a quiet notification shows it: "Rex is working", with the kind of step it is on, such as "Searching the web" or "Running a command", and how long it has been going.
The text goes through Google's push service, so every word is fixed but the teammate's name.
It never quotes the teammate's plan, the chat's title, a command, a file, a link, a search or what a tool was given: only the kind of step.
The chat shows the rest.
When the teammate made a plan, a progress bar shows how many of its steps are done; on Android 16 and newer this is Android's progress-style notification, with one segment per step.
While the teammate waits for you, the notification says so ("Rex is waiting for you", "Waiting for your approval") and turns amber; the approval or takeover request still arrives as its own notification, with its buttons.
It updates in place, at most every 15 seconds for new steps, without sound or vibration, and goes away when the work ends, replaced by the reply or failure notification when there is one.
Tapping it opens the run in its chat, and while that chat is on screen it stays hidden; it comes back, as it last was, when you leave the chat or press Home.
Swiping it away hides that run's progress until the run ends.
If it goes because nothing refreshed it for 30 minutes, that is no swipe: the run's next update shows it again.
Signing out removes every run's progress notification, with what the phone kept to draw them again, and the phone then draws no progress sent for the account you left, even after you sign in again.
On Android 14 and newer it is an ongoing notification, which you can still swipe away; on older versions, where an ongoing notification can't be swiped, it is a normal one.
It has its own channel, Running work, so you can turn it off or tune it in Android's settings without changing approvals, replies or the phone connection.
It puts no dot on the app's icon: a run in progress is nothing to read, and its result, which does, comes when it ends.

Which messages are urgent follows one rule.
Approvals, takeover requests, `egress-needed` and visible results (replies, automation results and failures) use high-priority FCM data messages, so Android starts the app's handler even while the app is closed.
`dismiss` and `run-progress` messages use normal priority.
A run's live progress updates collapse into the latest one at FCM (one collapse key per run), and each is best effort; its end has no collapse key, since FCM keeps only a few collapse keys per phone while it can't deliver, and may drop any of them.
Android deprioritizes apps whose high-priority messages show nothing, so a message that only updates or removes a notification never uses high priority.
Android's notification channels still control sound and alert prominence; delivery priority does not change those settings.

## Settings

Settings, from your initial on Home, has Light, Dark or System appearance, a switch for haptics, notifications, the [phone connection](#phone-connection), and security (app lock, password and passkeys, Always allowed rules, the [vault](#vault)).
Animations follow Android's Remove animations setting.

## The computer on your phone

Computer lists every teammate's screen, including sleeping screens, its current holder, and requests for you; screens that need you come first.
Each screen has one action right on the list: Wake, Watch, or Take over.
Watching a sleeping screen does not start it; use Wake or Take over.
The live viewer is noVNC bundled inside the app, with a fresh short-lived VNC token for every connection.
Your session cookie stays on the React Native side and is never passed to the WebView.

Tap to click, hold for a right click, and drag with two fingers to scroll.
Pinch zooms the local screen; Fit, which appears once the screen connects, resets it.
Use Trackpad for relative cursor movement and precise taps.
The keyboard bar provides Esc, Tab, Ctrl, Alt, arrows, Enter, and Paste from phone.
In portrait the screen fits the width; in landscape it fills the height beside the controls.
Rotating during takeover locks landscape until you hand back or leave the screen.
Back opens the hand-back sheet, where you can leave an optional note or stay in control.

Takeover requests open the screen from Home, chat, or a notification.
The notification offers Take over and Type the code.
Type the code is for a 4-to-8-digit one-time code, with a live preview and the focused site's name.
The app can request Android's consent to fill a code from one incoming SMS; it never requests SMS-reading permissions or sends the code without your tap.
Manual entry remains available if consent is denied, Play services is unavailable, or the SMS is ambiguous.

Code entry blocks the teammate's desktop tools, including screenshots, until botd verifies the code is gone from the browser's fields and page text.
If it is still visible, you keep control and can clear it before handing back.
An uncertain response is recovered by its operation id; the app never blindly repeats typing.
A code the computer refuses before acting (like while the worker and the computer run different versions during a deploy) fails with nothing typed, and the request stays open so you can send it again.
When the worker loses the computer's answer, it asks botd what became of that operation: botd answers as it did, waits for one still typing, or, for one it never got, refuses it from then on and says nothing was typed, so you can send the code again.
If botd typed and holds the screen for you, the takeover is recorded, and handing back answers the teammate's request; the screen is never reported handed back when nobody held it.
After a computer restart, affected screens stay paused; "Clear the code and hand back" navigates every browser page to `about:blank`, waits for each navigation to commit, and returns control only once every page is provably blank.
The submitted code is not stored in operation records, logs, or botd state.

## Tasks

The team's task board is the same one the web shows: To do, Doing and Done, shared by you and every teammate.
Home has a Tasks section under your teammates with the three open tasks that matter most: what waits on you, then what's overdue or due soon, then what's in progress.
A teammate's profile lists their open tasks, and Add a task there gives the new one to them.
Board, or All open, opens the whole board; search finds it as Tasks.
The tab bar already holds five tabs, so the board is a screen you open from these places rather than a sixth tab.

On the phone the columns stack: To do, then Doing, then Done, with what waits on you (an approval or a take over in the task's chat) first in each, and the four most recently finished tasks before Show more.
Filter by All, Mine (tasks without a teammate are yours) or Needs attention (waiting on you, or overdue).
Each task shows what holds it up, its title and notes, its owner's face (your initial for your own) and when it's due, amber once it's late.
The board refreshes on its own when you or a teammate changes a task, from the `tasks` app event.

Tap a task to edit its title, notes, owner, due day and status.
The task screen reads the task itself (`tasks.get`), so a task finished too long ago for the board still opens, and it says the task was deleted only when the server says so.
Only the fields you changed are sent, so a teammate's edit to another field meanwhile stays; leaving with unsaved changes asks first.
What you haven't touched follows the task as it changes elsewhere: if it's given to someone else on the web, the screen shows the new owner.
While a save is on its way the form is frozen, so nothing typed meanwhile is lost, and the chat link is disabled.
Ask to start (Ask again once the task was handed to that teammate) saves, hands the task to its teammate in their main chat, moves it to Doing, and opens that chat.
It asks the teammate the button names: if the task went to someone else meanwhile, it's refused and says who has it now.
Trying again after a failure sends the same start id, so an ask whose answer was lost isn't sent twice (see [the task board](/docs/flows/teamwork#the-task-board)).
A new task that was added but couldn't be started opens as itself, on its own task screen, so trying again starts it instead of adding it twice, and it has Status and Delete and says if it's deleted.
Where a save goes next (back, the chat, or the task's own screen) happens only if you're still on that screen, so leaving it while it saves never closes another one.
Hold a task, or use its menu button, to move it to another column or one place up or down in its own (the web's drag and drop), start it, open its chat, edit or delete it.
The menu shows the task as the board has it now, and closes if it's deleted.
A move is one `tasks.move` call that says where the task goes, not a position: at the end of another column, or just before the task drawn above it or after the one drawn below.
The worker places it against the whole column.
The board shows the move at once and keeps showing it until a board read after it has it; a refused move stops showing at once and you're told why, whether or not the board could be read again.
Move up and Move down step past the next task you can see: tasks hidden by the filter don't count, and a task never crosses the line between what waits on you and the rest, since that line decides the order first.
Deleting asks first, since the board has no undo, and says when the teammate may still be working on it.
While the phone is offline, everything that would change the board is disabled, and a task you're writing stays on screen until you're back.

## Automations

Automations lists what runs without you asking, as the web's Automations page does: schedules, then page watches, then Gmail and Calendar triggers, then webhooks.
Filter by teammate at the top; a teammate's profile opens it filtered to them, and search finds it as Automations.
Home has a Coming up section with the next three runs, and a teammate's profile has their daily check-in switch and up to four of their automations.
The check-in is the same quiet routine the web's switch turns on (see [quiet routines and check-ins](/docs/flows/automations#quiet-routines-and-check-ins)), so it isn't listed again under it; it stays the check-in when you rename or retime it.
Each row says when it runs or what it watches, how its last run or check went, and has a switch to pause or resume it; a reminder that already ran says Done and has no switch.
The list refreshes on its own from the `schedules` and `triggers` app events.

Tap one to see what it does, its recent runs, and Run now for a schedule or Check now for a watch or an account trigger.
Each run opens in its chat.
Run now sends an id the phone makes, and trying again soon after a lost answer sends the same id, so the schedule runs once.
The answer says whether that tap started the run or an earlier one already had, and the run in its history says you started it.
The id is forgotten when you leave the screen or two minutes after the last try, so a later tap starts a run of its own.
Check now says what it found, or why the check failed.
Both are off while the computer is offline, and every change is off while the phone is offline.
Delete asks first.

Edit changes the name and the timing: when a schedule runs (every day, weekdays, a day of the week, every hour, a cron expression, or once on a day at a time), how often a page watch looks, and how long before an event a Calendar trigger wakes its teammate.
It checks the timing with the same rules and words as the web's dialog, which share them through `packages/domain`.
A schedule's times are on its own clock, in its time zone: the cadence, the next run, Coming up, and "today" in the reminder's day picker.
A row names the zone ("Kolkata time") when it isn't the phone's, and the editor names it, and the phone's when they differ.
The editor says when a routine is quiet, and that making it a one-time reminder drops that; the daily check-in always repeats, so its editor has no Once.
Only what you changed is sent (`schedules.patch`, `triggers.patch`), so an edit made on the web meanwhile stays, and what you haven't touched follows the automation as it changes elsewhere.
Leaving with unsaved changes asks first, and a save goes back to the automation, also when the editor was opened from a link.
The prompt, the page and what to look for, the Gmail search, a webhook's URL and test, and making new automations stay on the web, and each screen says so.

## Memory

Memory is what your teammates remember: facts and preferences every teammate shares, and each teammate's own facts and lessons.
Open it from Settings, from a teammate's profile, or by searching for Memory.
The chips at the top switch between Shared and each teammate, and the search finds memories by their words, as on the web.
Each memory shows its type, who saved it (you, your feedback, or a teammate by name), and whether it's pinned, expires or was changed.
The list shows the newest 200; search to find older ones.

Tap a memory to edit what it says, its type (fact, preference, lesson or note) and whether teammates get it with every task.
An expiry set on the web shows there, and can be cleared; setting one is on the web.
Every change names the revision you opened (`expectedRevision`), so one made meanwhile on the web or by a teammate is refused (`STATE_CONFLICT`).
The screen then says "Changed since you opened it" with Reload, which shows the saved version; it never saves over the newer one.
Reload reads the memory from the server (`memory.get`): only a fresh copy replaces your changes, and when that read fails (offline, or the server failing) your changes and the conflict stay and the screen says why.
What you haven't touched follows the memory as it changes elsewhere.
A memory a teammate suggested shows Confirm and Dismiss; dismissing asks first, like Forget, which removes the memory and its history.
Forget checks the connection again once you confirm, and sends nothing if the phone went offline meanwhile.
When it's done, the screen leaves only once it's the one you're looking at, so History or a chat opened meanwhile stays open.
A memory forgotten elsewhere while you edit it says so; leaving still asks first while you have changes, and "Copy what you wrote" keeps your text.
History lists every revision with who made it and a link to the conversation, and Restore makes an earlier one the newest, as a new revision.
Once the server says the memory is gone, History shows that instead of revisions it read before, so nothing offers to restore it.

Add a memory with the plus.
Saving the same words again confirms or finds the one already saved instead of adding another (`saveOutcome`), and the screen says so.
Each new memory is sent with an id the phone makes for it (`memory.create`'s `id`), and the memory is saved under that id.
When a save gets no answer, the form stays as it was sent and Try again sends it unchanged, with the same id, so a lost save is never added twice.
If the first attempt was saved, the server answers with that memory as it is now, even if it was edited on the web since; if it was forgotten since, it says so (`STATE_CONFLICT`) and adding it again is a new save.
Leaving with unsaved changes asks first, and while the phone is offline nothing is sent and your draft stays.
Switching between Shared and a teammate shows a loading list, never the other scope's memories, while typing a search keeps the last results up, faded.
Each screen reads memory itself (`memory.get`, `memory.history`), so a link or a lesson opens it, and the `memory` app event refreshes it when anything changes.

A teammate's profile shows how much they remember, with suggestions to confirm, and their lessons: what they learned from your feedback, read before every task.
Lessons are memories of kind lesson, so each one opens to edit or forget.
When a teammate suggests new instructions for itself, Home lists it and its profile shows what would change, line by line, against the instructions as they are now (`proposals.pending`).
Accept or Dismiss says which is on its way, then that it's done; deciding the same way again is harmless, so a lost answer can be tried again, and one decided elsewhere says so.
Accept sends the instructions the change was shown against (`expectedInstructions`), so if you edited them on the web meanwhile, or archived the teammate, it's refused (`STATE_CONFLICT`) instead of overwriting them.
The card then says so and shows the change against the instructions as they are now, to review again.

## Pages

Pages are explainers, comparisons and dashboards your teammates build as HTML and publish for you.
A page a teammate publishes shows in the chat as a card with its picture; tap it to open the version it published.
Home lists the latest pages under your teammates, and All pages opens the full list, pinned ones first.
Hold a page to pin it to the top or delete it.
Search finds pages by their title and summary.

A page opens in a viewer that loads it from open-bot's separate pages service, with a link that works for a few minutes.
Your session cookie is never passed to that WebView, cookies and storage are off, and the page can't reach the network.
Links in a page open in a browser tab, never inside the viewer.
The viewer follows the app's light or dark appearance and gets a fresh link when that changes or when an old link has run out.
Tap the title to switch versions, and when a teammate publishes a newer version while you read, a banner offers it.
Select text in a page and tap Ask about this, or use the Ask button in the header, to ask its teammate a question or for a change.
The question goes to the chat the page was made in, or the teammate's main chat, with the page, version and passage attached.
The menu also opens the page in the browser, goes to its chat, pins it or deletes it.
A notification about a published page opens the page.

## Vault

The vault holds the logins and cards your teammates use without ever seeing them (see [Vault and payments](/docs/flows/vault-and-payments)).
Open it from Settings, by searching for Vault, or from Spending on a teammate's profile.
Opening it asks for your fingerprint or the phone's PIN every time, even right after you unlocked the app.
It stays open while you move between its screens, and locks again when you leave the last of them, when the app goes to the background, or after five minutes.
Leaving counts once no vault screen has been in front for a second, so a chat opened from a purchase leaves the vault even though the budget screen is still under it.
A locked vault covers the screen, and nothing on it can be changed until you open it again; what you were typing stays.
What's under the cover is hidden from TalkBack and takes no touches, and the header doesn't name the item.
A phone without a screen lock can't open the vault, and the screen says to set one up.

The list shows each login with its username and site, and each card with its brand and last four digits, with who may use it and when it was last used.
It never shows a password, card number or security code: the phone never receives one, and no procedure returns one.
Add a login or a card with its section's Add, and choose which teammates may use it, or every teammate.
Tap an item to rename it, change its site or who may use it, or replace a secret by typing a new one; a secret field left empty keeps what's saved.
An edit sends only what you changed (`vault.patch`), so a change made on the web meanwhile to something else stays.
What you haven't touched follows the item as it changes elsewhere, and an item deleted elsewhere while you edit it says so.
Delete asks first; the item leaves the list at once and comes back if the delete fails.
Leaving with unsaved changes asks first, and while the phone is offline nothing is sent.

Budgets lists each teammate with what they spent this month.
Tap one to choose whether they may pay for things, their monthly budget and the amount approved without asking, and to see their purchases; a purchase opens the chat it was made in.
Saving names the budget your changes started from (`teammates.setBudget`'s `expected`), so a budget saved on the web meanwhile is refused (`STATE_CONFLICT`) rather than overwritten.
While you edit, a field you haven't touched follows a budget changed elsewhere.
When it changed a field you changed too, to something else, the screen says what it was changed to ("Changed elsewhere to $80 a month"), keeps your value, and waits until you use theirs or keep yours before saving.
The web's Spending tab works the same way, with the same rules (`packages/domain/src/vault/budget.ts`).
The `vault` and `purchases` app events refresh both.

What you type into the vault lives only on the screen you type it on: it is never kept as a draft or cached, and it is cleared once saved and when you leave.
While a vault screen is in front, the app is kept out of screenshots, screen recordings and the recent apps preview (Android's `FLAG_SECURE`, set by the app's own `modules/secure-screen`, which needs no permission), and the fields turn off autofill and the keyboard's suggestions.
A password you show uses the visible-password keyboard, which learns nothing.
The list and budgets the phone read are dropped when you leave the vault.
A new item is sent under an id the phone makes for it (`vault.create`).
When its save gets no answer, the form stays as it was sent and Try again sends it unchanged, with the same id, so it is never added twice.

## Phone connection

Websites often block cloud computers, so teammates' browser can go online through your own devices instead.
The phone can carry that traffic when your Mac is offline, or always, if you choose.
A native foreground service runs the same egress agent as the desktop app, without starting React Native, and a high-priority push wakes it when the computer needs a connection.
How it works underneath (modes, waking, the device credential) is in [Desktop app and egress](/docs/architecture/desktop-and-egress#your-phone-as-a-connection).

### Turning it on

Open Settings → Phone connection.
While it's off, the screen explains what it costs: pages teammates load use the phone's data (Wi-Fi only unless you allow mobile data), it runs while teammates need it (or all the time, if you choose Always), and some sites ask teammates to sign in again when traffic moves between your Mac, your phone and the cloud.
Use this phone's connection pairs the phone.
It first checks that notifications are allowed, including the Phone connection channel, because without them you would never see "Tap to start" or why it paused; if either is off, it says so and opens Android's settings for it.
If phones were set to Never, pairing sets them to When my Mac is offline.

### While it's on

The screen shows where teammates' browser goes online right now ("Through your Mac", "Through your phone", "Direct from the cloud", "Waiting for your phone" or "Browsing paused", with why), what this phone is doing (carrying traffic, standing by, ready, paused and why, or stopped by you), and the data it used today and this month, with how much of it was mobile data.
Start now and Stop start or stop it by hand; after Stop, teammates can't wake it for 15 minutes, and in Always it stays off until you start it.

- **When it helps**: When my Mac is offline, Always (before my Mac), or Never. This applies to every paired phone, and the web's Settings → Browsing changes the same setting.
- **Rules for this phone**: Wi-Fi only, a monthly mobile data limit (2 GB unless you pick another), and pausing below 20% battery while not charging. Each pause posts a short notification saying why.
- **Paired phones**: every phone with its last use; Remove unpairs another one.
- **Turn off for this phone** stops the service, forgets its credential and unpairs it.

If a wake goes unanswered and Android may be holding the app back to save battery, the screen offers Allow in background, and on Xiaomi phones also says to turn on Autostart.
If Low Power Standby is on, it says that too, since Android cuts the connection while the screen is off.
When browsing through your devices is off altogether, a notice offers to turn it on.
A "Your phone didn't answer" notification opens this screen.

The Computer tab shows the same path at the top, and tapping it opens Phone connection.

### Signing out

Signing out stops the phone connection first: the service stops and the phone forgets its credential before anything else is cleared.
Then the app unpairs the phone while the session still works; if it can't reach the server, it remembers to, for that pairing only, and does it once you're signed in again.
The same goes for Turn off for this phone while offline.
A phone removed from the web or another phone forgets its pairing the next time its Phone connection screen loads the list, or when it next tries to connect.
A session that ends elsewhere (a password change on the web, for example), or expires, signs the phone out the same way, once the server says there is no session; while the phone is offline or the server fails, nothing is cleared.
Until then, the worker already sends nothing to a phone whose session has expired or ended.

## Sharing to a teammate

Share a link or text from any app (like a LinkedIn post) and pick open-bot to send it to a teammate.
Choose Make a page explaining this (which runs the built-in /page skill), Summarize it for me, or Just send it, add an optional note, and pick who gets it.
The message goes to that teammate's main chat, and the chat opens.
A share waits until you're signed in and past the app lock.
