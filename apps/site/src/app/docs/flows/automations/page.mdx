# Automations

Automations wake a teammate without you.
**Schedules** fire at a time, **page watches** fire when a web page changes, and **webhooks** fire when another service calls a URL.
With [your accounts](/docs/flows/accounts) connected, **Gmail** triggers fire when new mail arrives, and **Calendar** triggers fire before your events.
They all end the same way: a queued run in the teammate's main chat.

## Where automations run

Every automation's run goes to the teammate's [main chat](/docs/concepts#chat), so what a teammate did on its own is next to your conversation with it, and you can reply to it there.
Each automation works in its own lane of that chat: `schedule:<id>` for a schedule and `trigger:<id>` for a page watch, webhook, Gmail or Calendar trigger.
A lane has its own Codex thread, so an automation remembers its earlier runs, and its work never runs inside your conversation's turn.
See [Threads](/docs/architecture/agent-runtime#threads).

The next time you talk to the teammate, your turn starts with what its automations finished that it hasn't told your conversation yet: up to 5 results, each cut at 1,500 characters, oldest first.
That way, when you answer "do the first one", the teammate knows what you are looking at.
If one of those results came from reading your accounts, your turn counts as having read outside data too (see [Your accounts](/docs/flows/accounts#mail-events-and-pages-are-not-instructions)).

An automation's run can't rename the main chat; only your conversation can.
Older teammates may still have a Scheduled chat (`chats.kind = 'scheduled'`) from before; it stays as history, and no new runs go there.

Because runs are just rows, automations keep working with your laptop closed.
They need the worker and the computer to be running, not the web app or a browser.

## Who creates them

You create automations on the Automations page, or on a teammate's Automations tab.
Teammates create them too, with their own tools, for themselves or, with `bot_id`, for another teammate:

| Tool                | Creates                                 |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| `schedule_task`     | A cron routine or a one-time reminder   |
| `watch_page`        | A page watch                            |
| `create_webhook`    | A webhook URL                           |
| `watch_gmail`       | A Gmail trigger                         |
| `watch_calendar`    | A Calendar trigger                      |
| `list_automations`  | Lists its automations with their ids    |
| `update_automation` | Pauses, resumes, renames or changes one |
| `cancel_automation` | Deletes one                             |

Without `bot_id`, a teammate sees and changes only its own automations.
With it, the automation must belong to that teammate, and setting it up may ask you first: see [Building the team](/docs/flows/teamwork#what-asks-you).

## Schedules

A schedule is a row in `schedules`:

- `kind`: `cron` for a recurring routine, `at` for a one-time reminder;
- `cron` and `timezone`, or a single time;
- `prompt`, the instructions the teammate gets;
- `next_run_at`, computed when the schedule is saved and after every run;
- `enabled`, and `quiet` (below).

### The scheduler

`ScheduleService` (`apps/worker/src/services/schedules.ts`) ticks every 10 seconds, and right away when a schedule is created or changed (the web app sends `NOTIFY openbot_worker, 'schedules'`).

Each tick runs in one transaction:

```sql title="Claiming due schedules"
select * from schedules
where enabled = true and next_run_at <= now()
for update skip locked
```

For each due row, it computes the next run time with `cron-parser` in the schedule's time zone (a reminder gets none and is disabled), saves it, and then queues a run:

```text title="The run's prompt"
[Scheduled task: "Morning briefing"]

Check my calendar and inbox, and tell me what needs me today.
```

Computing the next time before queueing means a crash between the two can skip one occurrence, but can never fire one twice.
If the worker was down for a while, a schedule fires once when it comes back, not once per missed occurrence.

Each schedule's recent runs are listed under it, and **Run now** fires it without changing its next run time.
A paused routine stays paused when you edit it, and a finished reminder moved to a new time runs again.

## Quiet routines and check-ins

A schedule can be **quiet**.
Its prompt gets one more line:

> Only tell the user about things worth their attention. If there's nothing, reply with exactly: Nothing to report.

When a quiet routine's run replies exactly that, it is marked `runs.quiet`.
It sends no notification, stays out of the briefing on Home, doesn't mark the chat unread, move it up the list or become its preview, isn't passed on to your next turn, and shows as one muted line in the chat.
Every template starts with a quiet daily check-in, and a teammate's Automations tab can turn one on.

## Page watches

A page watch checks a URL on an interval, from 5 minutes to a week, and wakes the teammate when the page changes.
It is a row in `triggers` with `kind = 'watch'`, the URL, the interval, optional text to look for, and the page's last text.

### Checking

`TriggerService` (`apps/worker/src/services/triggers.ts`) ticks every 30 seconds and checks up to 4 watches at a time.
A check:

- fetches from the worker, without a browser, cookies or sign-ins, with a 20-second timeout;
- follows at most 5 redirects, checking each one;
- reads at most 3 MB;
- refuses private, loopback, link-local and metadata addresses when `WATCH_ALLOW_PRIVATE_URLS` is false (the default in production), so a watch can't be used to probe the services next to the worker.

### Comparing

`pageText()` reduces HTML to readable text: comments, scripts, styles, SVGs and iframes are dropped, block-level tags become line breaks, entities are decoded, whitespace is collapsed, and the result is capped at 200,000 characters.
One block per line makes two versions easy to compare.

`watchChange()` then decides whether to fire:

- The **first** check never fires. It only learns what the page looks like.
- A watch **without** text to look for fires on any added or removed line. Lines are compared as multisets, so moving content around without changing it doesn't fire.
- A watch **with** text to look for (like "In stock") fires only on the check where that text **appears**, not again while it stays.

The run's prompt has your instructions, then up to 40 added and 40 removed lines (each cut at 300 characters), introduced as data from outside, not instructions.

## Webhooks

A webhook is a private URL that starts a teammate when anything calls it:

```text
https://<your-app>/api/hooks/<token>
```

- The token (20 to 64 URL-safe characters) is the only credential. Anyone who knows the URL can start the teammate, so treat it like a password.
- `GET`, `POST` and `PUT` are accepted, with a body of at most 256 KB.
- Each call queues a run with the method, the query and the body (JSON is pretty-printed, and the payload is cut at 20,000 characters), introduced as outside data.
- Each webhook starts at most **30 runs an hour**. Calls over the limit get an error.
- **New URL** rotates the token, and the old URL stops working at once.

The public route is in the web app, which checks the size and forwards the request to the worker's `POST /internal/hooks/:token`.
The response is `202 Accepted` with the run id.

```sh title="Calling a webhook"
curl -X POST https://<your-app>/api/hooks/<token> \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"event":"deploy_failed","service":"api"}'
```

## Mail and calendar

These watch your own Gmail or Google Calendar, through a connection the teammate may read.
You create them on the Automations page, under **New automation**, and they are listed together under **Mail and calendar**.
A teammate creates them with `watch_gmail` and `watch_calendar`.

### New mail

A Gmail trigger has a search, in Gmail's own syntax, like `from:school.org` or `subject:invoice has:attachment`, or none for all new mail.
Mail you send, drafts and chats never count, and `older_than` and `before` can't be used, since the trigger is about new mail.

- It checks every 2 minutes. A new or resumed trigger starts from the mailbox as it is, so mail that was already there doesn't wake the teammate.
- Each check starts one run with all the new mail that matches: up to 10 messages in full, and any others listed by subject.
- A message never wakes the same trigger twice.

`AccountTriggers` (`apps/worker/src/services/account-triggers.ts`) checks all the triggers on one mailbox with one Gmail `history.list` request, from the oldest trigger's place in the mailbox, and each trigger takes what is newer than its own.
New messages are matched with the trigger's search in parentheses, plus `-in:sent -in:drafts -in:chats newer_than:2d`, so an `OR` in your search can't undo the rest.
If Gmail no longer has the history, after a long outage, the trigger looks at the last day's mail instead, and skips mail older than the trigger itself.

```text title="The run's prompt"
[Gmail: "Receipts"]

File every receipt in the Receipts label.

A new email arrived in you@gmail.com (mail matching “subject:receipt”). …

From Gmail (you@gmail.com): the new message. …
<outside_data>
--- message 18f2… in thread 18f2…
From: Shop <orders@shop.example>
Subject: Your receipt
…
</outside_data>
```

### Upcoming events

A Calendar trigger wakes the teammate a set time before each event on your primary calendar, from when it starts to a week before, for every event or only events with other people on them.

- It checks every 5 minutes, and each event starts its own run, with the event's time, place, guests and description.
- Each occurrence of a repeating event counts, once. An event that moves to a new time wakes it again for the new time.
- Cancelled events, all-day events and events you declined don't count.

An event is due when its wake-up time is within half a check, and it didn't start more than 10 minutes ago, so a late check still catches it.

### Limits and safety

- Each Gmail or Calendar trigger starts at most **30 runs an hour**. A check over the limit shows why on the trigger.
- What woke a trigger is kept in `trigger_firings`, keyed by the message (`mail:<id>`) or the occurrence and its start (`event:<id>@<start>`), so it can't fire twice, even from two workers.
- Their runs start as having read your accounts, so anything they would send out, like an email or a reply to an invitation, asks you first. See [Your accounts](/docs/flows/accounts#mail-events-and-pages-are-not-instructions).
- If the account needs reconnecting, you're told once, and the trigger shows the problem until you reconnect it. **Check now** shows the reason too.

## Prompt injection

Watched pages, webhook payloads, and the mail and events that wake a teammate come from outside, and may contain text that looks like instructions.
Both are wrapped in a note that says to treat them as data (`PAGE_TEXT_NOTE` and `WEBHOOK_PAYLOAD_NOTE`), and the developer instructions repeat the rule.
This lowers the risk, but can't remove it.
Teammates still need your approval for consequential actions according to their autonomy level, and that is the real safeguard.
Runs started by mail or an event go further: everything they would send out asks you, whatever the teammate's autonomy.
